About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science with a dual focus on formal language theory and natural language processing (NLP). My research spans M-unambiguous language, language sparsity, and probability convergence, as well as advanced NLP techniques like self-training for few-shot learning and grammar-based data augmentation. I am set to graduate soon and have contributed to several publications in both theoretical computer science and NLP applications.

Research

My research interests include:

  • M-equivalence, strong M-equivalence, and language sparsity
  • Formal language theory and computational complexity
  • Natural language processing (NLP) with a focus on few-shot learning and self-training techniques
  • Grammar-based data augmentation and its application in text classification
  • Parikh matrix mapping and its relevance to computational linguistics
